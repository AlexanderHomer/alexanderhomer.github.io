## Demographics

* endoscopic sinus surgery and NSGY are most common iaotrogenic causes
* obese females with elevated ICP in the lateral sphenoid recess in spontaneous leaks
* Other common sites: fovea ethmoidalis (posteromedial region), lateral lamella, cribiform plate, sphenoid sinus

## Kero’s Classification




1. Type I - depth of 1-3mm
2. Type II - depth of 4-7mm
3. Type III - depth of 8-16mm

### Further Classification

* extra vs intradural
* intra vs extra arachnoid
* low vs high flow
  * cistern violation

## Treatment

* Small defect (<1cm) - acellular/nonvascular tissue graft
* Medium defect (1-3cm) - Local vascularized tissue flap
* Large (>3cm) - regional/free flap construction

### Patient Factors

* Radiation
* Smoking
* Elevated ICP
* Obesity
* OSA


### Steps

* expose defect in 360 degree manner, strip sinus mucosa to prevent mucocele formation
* Reduce encephalocele if present with bipolar cautery and multilary closure
  * Multilayr closure involves both inlay and overlay grafts
    * Inlay is between the dura and the skill base
      * Includes abdominal fat, acellular dermis, fascia lata
    * Onlay is in place extracranially
      * Includes mucosal grafts, pedicle vascular flap
* Small defects (<1cm) can use any multilayer reconstruction with >90% sucess
* Regional vaascular flaps have multiple benefits, including low donor site morbidity, decreased compliations, and acelerated healing times.


### **Local/Regional Flaps**

According to *Raza Pasha & Golub – Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery: Clinical Reference Guide* (pp. 317–318, 459) and *Bailey’s Head & Neck Surgery*:

* **Pericranial (or Galeopericranial) Flap**
  * **Type:** Vascularized fascia flap from the frontal scalp, based on the **supraorbital and supratrochlear arteries**.
  * **Use:** Commonly used for **anterior skull base reconstruction** following craniofacial resection or CSF leak repair.
  * **Advantages:** Excellent vascularity; large surface area; easily harvested via bicoronal incision; watertight dural support.
  * **Limitations:** Requires external incision; not ideal for endoscopic access.
* **Temporalis Muscle Flap**
  * **Type:** Regional myofascial flap, pedicled on the **deep temporal arteries**.
  * **Use:** Reconstruction of anterior or lateral skull base; effective for dural reinforcement.
  * **Advantages:** Readily available, robust vascularity.
  * **Limitations:** May produce temporal hollowing or limited reach medially.
* **Temporoparietal Fascia Flap (TPFF)**
  * **Type:** Fasciocutaneous flap based on the **superficial temporal artery**.
  * **Use:** Reconstruction of **anterior skull base** and upper/midface defects.
  * **Advantages:** Thin, pliable, vascularized tissue ideal for endoscopic transposition; minimal donor morbidity.
  * **Limitations:** Risk of alopecia or injury to the temporal branch of the facial nerve.
* **Fascia Lata or Free Grafts (nonvascularized)**
  * Used for smaller defects or layered closure under vascularized tissue.

### **Endoscopic (Intranasal) Options**

While *Bailey’s Section II (Rhinology)* does not detail these explicitly, standard endoscopic skull base practice includes:

* **Hadad–Bassagasteguy (Nasoseptal) Flap**
  * **Type:** Pedicled mucoperiosteal flap based on the **posterior septal branch of the sphenopalatine artery**.
  * **Use:** Primary choice for **endoscopic anterior skull base CSF leak repair**.
  * **Advantages:** Large surface area, dependable vascularity, low failure rate.
  * **Limitations:** Cannot be used if prior septal surgery or radiation compromised supply.
* **Inferior Turbinate Flap / Middle Turbinate Flap**
  * For **small anterior defects** when nasoseptal flap unavailable

### **Free Tissue Transfer**

For **large or composite anterior cranial base defects**:

* **Radial forearm**, **anterolateral thigh (ALT)**, or **rectus abdominis** free flaps can be used when local or regional options are insufficient for bulk or vascular reliability.


### Complications

* Pneumocephalus, meningitis, local infection, failure of flap/graft
